Links on your site that lead to "Page not found": the fix

A local auto shop rebuilt its website. The new pages looked great, but the menu, the footer and a couple of buttons still pointed at the old page addresses. A customer who tapped Financing or Coupons got a bare "Page not found" and, most of the time, left. The owner never saw it: they always came in through the home page, and the pages they checked by hand were the new ones. Here is what each link did before and after, and the fix.

What was wrong

A redesign gives pages new addresses. The pages were moved, but the links pointing at them, and the outside world's links to the old addresses, were not. Nothing was "down", so no alarm went off: each visitor just met a dead end on their own.

Before:
  /auto-loans/      404   (page now lives at /financing/)
  /specials.html    404   (never rebuilt)

After:
  /auto-loans/      301 -> /financing/
  /specials.html    301 -> /coupons/
  anything else     404 page with phone, hours and the menu

How it was fixed

  1. List every dead link. A crawl of the whole site finds each link that answers "not found", and the page it was linked from, so nothing is missed and nothing is guessed.
  2. Decide each one. Point the link at the page that replaced it, bring back a page that should never have gone, or remove the link. Each choice is written down for you before anything changes.
  3. Forward the old addresses (a "301 redirect") to their new pages, so Google, old posts and bookmarks keep working. WordPress, Wix, Squarespace and Shopify all have a redirects screen for this.
  4. Make the "not found" page useful: your phone number, hours and the menu, so the rare visitor who still lands there can carry on.
  5. Crawl again on a phone and a desktop: zero dead links, and each old address ends on the right page.
