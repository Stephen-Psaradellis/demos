The Website button on your Google listing goes to the wrong place

People find you on Google Maps or in the box beside the search results, tap Website, and land on an old domain, a "page not found", a Facebook page you stopped updating, or a site that is not yours. Many of them never try again. Your own site can be perfectly fine and still lose those visits. Below is a rebuilt example (not a real client), the two-minute check, and the fix for each cause.

The two-minute check


    
  
  1. Test it as a customer. On your phone, signed out or in a private window, search your business name, tap Website, and note the exact address it opens. Do it on Google Maps too: the same profile feeds both.
    2. 
  
  2. Open the profile as the owner. Signed in to the Google account that manages it, search your business name; your profile tools appear at the top. Choose Edit profile > Contact and read the Website field. If you see no tools at all, the profile is managed by another Google account (row 4).
    3. 
  
  3. Look for a notice from Google. A banner such as "Google updated your profile" or a pending edit means the link was changed by Google or a suggested edit (row 3).
    4. 
  
  4. Count the profiles. Search Google Maps for your business name and street. Two pins for the same business means the one customers see may not be the one you edit (row 5).
    5. 




What causes it and the fix





  
What you seeWhy it happensThe fix

  
The button opens an address ending in .business.site and shows "page not found"Google closed the free websites it built from Business Profiles in March 2024; the links redirected to the profile for a few months and then stopped working. Many listings still point at themPut your real site's address in Edit profile > Contact > Website. No site yet? A one-page site on your own domain beats a dead link; until then, clear the field rather than leave it broken

  
It opens your old domain, or a site from a web designer you no longer useYou moved the site and nobody changed the profile. If the old domain still redirects, customers get there in the end; if it lapsed, they get an error or someone else's pageChange the Website field to the new address, exactly as it loads in your browser (with https://). Keep the old domain renewed and redirecting for a year if you can: old links all over the web still use it

  
You set it correctly once, and now it is differentAnyone can suggest an edit to a business on Google, and Google also changes details it believes are out of date. Unreviewed changes can go liveIn the profile, review the Google update and choose to keep your own value, then save the correct link again. Check the profile once a month; a business with an active owner gets changed less

  
You cannot find any edit tools, or Google says the profile is managed by someone elseA former agency, web designer or employee claimed it with their own Google accountAsk them to add you as an Owner under the profile's managers. If they will not or cannot, use Google's Request access on the profile; if there is no answer, Google may let you verify it yourself. Only you, the business, should do this, never a third party

  
You edited the link, but customers still see the old oneThere are two profiles for one business (an old address, a second phone number, a former name), and customers are shown the one you do not manage. Or your edit is still being reviewedGive an edit up to a few days. For a duplicate, on the extra pin choose Suggest an edit > Close or remove > Duplicate (or ask Google support from your managed profile) so only one remains

  
The Website link is right, but Order, Book or Menu sends people to another companyThose are separate links. Some are added by ordering or booking services that list your business, not by youIn Edit profile, set your own links for menu, ordering and booking, and mark them as preferred where Google offers it. Ask a service you no longer use to remove its listing

  
The link opens your site, but the site itself shows an errorThe profile is fine; the site is down, expired or showing a security warningFix the site first: see site can't be reached and "Not secure" warning







What not to do


    
  
  • Do not create a new profile to "start fresh". You get a duplicate, the two split your reviews, and either can be shown.
    • 
  
  • Do not point the Website button at a Facebook or directory page when you have a working site. Those pages are harder to keep current and show other businesses next to yours.
    • 
  
  • Do not let an agency own the profile on its account. Have them added as a manager on yours.
    •
