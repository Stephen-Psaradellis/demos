Website down: "This site can't be reached"

You type your business address and get a grey error page, a "this domain has expired" page full of ads, or a plain "Account Suspended". Customers see the same thing, and often your business email has stopped too. The error code under the message says which of four things broke: the domain, its DNS records, the hosting account, or the server. Each has a different fix, and two of them get harder to undo the longer they wait. Below is a rebuilt example (not a real client), how to read the code, and the fix for each.

The causes and their fixes





  
CauseHow to confirm itWhat to do

  
The domain expiredLook the domain up at lookup.icann.org: an expiry date in the past, or a status of clientHold, redemptionPeriod or pendingDelete. It also names the registrar (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace Domains...)Log in at that registrar and renew it, then turn on auto-renew and update the card and the contact email. Right after expiry a normal renewal usually brings it back within an hour or a few. Wait longer and many registrars move it to a redemption period with a much higher fee, and after that the name can be released for anyone to buy. Exact days vary by registrar and by ending (.com, .co.uk...), so act the same day

  
Nameservers or DNS records changedThe domain is paid, but the ICANN lookup shows nameservers you do not recognise, or dnschecker.org shows no A record, or an address that is not your host's. Common after a hosting move, a registrar transfer, or a "connect your domain" step in a site builderSet the nameservers back to your host's (your host's help pages list them), or put back the A record and the www CNAME the host gives you. Copy the MX records too, or email stops. Changes take from minutes to a day to reach everyone

  
Hosting account suspended"Account Suspended" on every page, and an email from the host. Check the host's billing page and inbox, including spamPay the open invoice or update the card; the host usually switches it back on the same day. If the reason was malware, the host wants it cleaned first: see the hacked-redirect guide

  
The server is down or overloadedTimes out for everyone (phone on mobile data too); the host's status page reports an incident, or the site fails every afternoon when traffic peaksCheck the host's status page and open a ticket with the exact time and error. If it keeps happening, the plan has run out of room or something is hammering it: see the 502/504 guide and the 429 guide

  
Only you are blockedFails on your office Wi-Fi, loads on mobile data. Often right after several wrong passwords: a security plugin (Wordfence, iThemes) or the host's firewall blocked your internet addressAsk the host to unblock your address (search "what is my IP" and send it), or unblock it in the security plugin from another connection. If nothing was blocked, restart the router, or on Windows run ipconfig /flushdns







Things that also stop when the domain lapses


    
  
  • Email at the domain. If your address is you@yourbusiness.com, it stops receiving with the website. Customers' emails bounce. Renewing fixes both.
    • 
  
  • Your Google listing's website link. Google keeps showing it, and every click lands on the error. Once it is back, open the link from the listing to check.
    • 
  
  • Payment and booking links that live on the site, and the confirmation emails they send.
    • 




What not to do


    
  
  • Do not buy a "new" domain with a different ending and start over. Every link, review site and printed card uses the old one. Renew it, even late.
    • 
  
  • Do not pay anyone who emails offering to "recover" or "restore" your expired domain. Renew only by logging in to the registrar the ICANN lookup names.
    • 
  
  • Do not change nameservers to "test". If you do not know the old values, write them down before touching anything; a wrong change can take a day to undo.
    • 
  
  • Do not let a suspension sit. Some hosts delete a suspended account's files after a few weeks.
    •
