Contact form spam: stop the flood, keep the real enquiries

The website form sends you forty messages a day: link lists in Russian, "I can get you to page one of Google", crypto offers, blank names with random letters. Somewhere in there is the one customer who wanted a quote, and you missed them. Most of this is bots that find the form and post to it directly; the rest is people paid to paste sales pitches. Each kind has a different fix, and the right fix stops nearly all of it without making real customers solve puzzles. Below is a rebuilt example (not a real client), how to tell which kind you have, and what to do.

Website enquiries inbox, Tuesday 10:15

What the checks found


  





First: look at ten of them


    
  
  1. Note what the junk looks like. Random letters in the name field, a message that is only links, or the same text arriving every few minutes: that is a bot. A readable, polite sales pitch (SEO, web design, "business funding") with a real-looking name: that is a person, or a bot that types like one.
    2. 
  
  2. Check the form for protection. Open the form's settings in your form plugin (Contact Form 7, WPForms, Gravity Forms, Elementor, Wix or Squarespace's own forms). If there is no honeypot, no CAPTCHA and no anti-spam option switched on, that is the first row below and the fix is ten minutes.
    3. 
  
  3. Check whether the form emails the visitor. A "send me a copy" box or an automatic "thanks, we got your message" email to the address typed in means anyone can make your site send mail to anyone. Spammers use that to deliver their message to strangers from your domain, and your own emails start landing in spam (the fourth row).
    4. 




The kinds of spam and their fixes





  
What it isHow to spot itWhat to do

  
Simple bots, and the form has no protectionGibberish names, link-only messages, bursts at night, sometimes hundreds a day. The form settings show no honeypot or CAPTCHASwitch on the plugin's own honeypot or anti-spam option first: Gravity Forms "Enable anti-spam honeypot" in Form Settings, WPForms "Enable anti-spam protection", Elementor Pro's Honeypot field. Then add Cloudflare Turnstile (free, no puzzle for most visitors, works whether or not your site uses Cloudflare) through the plugin's CAPTCHA or Integration page

  
Bots that get past a CAPTCHAAn old reCAPTCHA (v2 checkbox or v3) is on, but junk still arrives, often posted straight to the form's address without loading the pageReplace it with Turnstile or hCaptcha, keep the honeypot alongside it, and make sure the plugin checks the token on the server (it does when the integration is set up in the plugin, not pasted in as page code)

  
People pasting sales pitchesReadable English, a real-looking name and email, the same SEO or "funding" offer sent to thousands of sites. CAPTCHAs do not stop these, because a person solves themContent filtering: Akismet checks each message against what it has seen across millions of sites (Contact Form 7 uses it through field options such as akismet:author_email; free for personal sites, a business site needs a paid plan). Contact Form 7 also blocks words listed in Settings > Discussion > Disallowed Comment Keys, so add the phrases that keep coming back. A "How did you hear about us?" or "What service?" dropdown that is required also cuts it, because pitch-pasters skip forms that need thought

  
Your form is being used to send spam to othersBounces and "who are you?" replies from people you never wrote to; your site's emails start going to spam. The form has a copy-to-sender box or an auto-reply to the typed addressTurn off copy-to-sender and the auto-reply, or send it only after a CAPTCHA passes. Never put the visitor's address in the From field: send from your own address and put theirs in Reply-To. If your mail already lands in spam, see site emails not arriving

  
Junk sign-ups, comments or orders, not just the formNew user accounts with random names, comment spam, WooCommerce "card testing" orders of $1Turn off "Anyone can register" in Settings > General unless you need it, close comments on old posts, and add the same Turnstile check to the checkout (most payment plugins and anti-spam plugins offer it). Many fake orders at once is card testing: tell your payment provider







Check it worked


Send yourself a real enquiry from your phone, on mobile data, through the live form: it should arrive, with your name and message intact. Then watch the inbox for two days. Most plugins keep blocked messages in a log or a spam folder (Flamingo for Contact Form 7, WPForms Entries, Gravity Forms' Spam list): look through it once, so a real customer caught by mistake gets a reply.



What not to do


    
  
  • Do not delete the form and put a bare email address on the page instead. Address-harvesting bots pick it up and the spam moves to your inbox, with no filter in front of it.
    • 
  
  • Do not stack three CAPTCHAs and a math question. Real customers on phones give up; the honeypot plus Turnstile stops the bots without them noticing.
    • 
  
  • Do not block whole countries at the firewall to stop form spam. The bots run from cloud servers in every country, and you lose real visitors.
    • 
  
  • Do not reply to the pitches, even to say no. A reply marks your address as read by a person.
    • 



For most sites this is a honeypot switch, one free Turnstile key and a look at the auto-reply, about twenty minutes once you know which kind of spam it is.
