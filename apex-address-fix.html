Your address without the www fails: one DNS record

A local shop's site opened fine at www.example-shop.com, which is what the owner always clicked from a bookmark. But most people type the short name, example-shop.com, from a business card, a van or a Google listing, and that showed an error page. Nothing on the site itself was broken: the short name simply was not pointed anywhere. Here is what each address did before and after, and the fix.

What was wrong

The domain's DNS (the address book that tells browsers where a name lives) had an entry for www and none for the bare name. That happens a lot after a move to a new website company: the new host's setup page asks you to add the www record, and the old record for the bare name is deleted or left pointing at the old server.

Before:
  www              CNAME   shops.example-host.com
  (bare name)      -- nothing --

After:
  www              CNAME   shops.example-host.com
  @  (bare name)   A       the host's address, from its setup page
                           (or the domain company's forwarding to www)

How it was fixed

  1. Find who holds the DNS. Not always the website company: often the domain company (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace Domains) or Cloudflare. A public lookup shows which, in a minute.
  2. Ask the website company what the bare name should point at. Every host publishes it (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify and most hosting panels show the exact record on their "connect a domain" page).
  3. Add that one record for the bare name, or turn on the domain company's forwarding from the bare name to the www address. Nothing else in the DNS is touched, so email keeps working.
  4. Make sure the certificate covers both names, so the short address does not open on a "Not secure" warning. Most hosts issue it on their own within an hour of the record appearing; some need one click.
  5. Check every version: bare and www, http and https, on a phone on mobile data and on a desktop. Each one should end on the same home page with the padlock on.
