Your website's emails land in spam or junk

The contact form works, orders go through, password resets are sent, but customers find the email in their spam or junk folder, or Gmail shows a red "Be careful with this message" banner. Since 2024 Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook check three DNS records on your domain (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) before trusting a message. Mail that fails them is filed as spam, or refused. Here is how to read the verdict and fix it.

What the customer sees

mail.google.com - inbox

What the checks found


  





Why it happened (five minutes)


    
  
  1. Read the verdict Gmail wrote. Send a test from your site to a Gmail address, open it, then the three dots, "Show original". The top lines say SPF: PASS or FAIL, DKIM and DMARC. Each FAIL is one reason it went to spam.
    2. 
  
  2. Who actually sent it? By default WordPress sends with PHP mail() from your web host's server, while the From line says your domain. To Gmail that is a stranger's server using your name, so SPF fails.
    3. 
  
  3. Is there an SPF record? Look up your domain's TXT records (any DNS lookup tool, or your DNS host's panel). One record should start v=spf1 and name every service that sends for you. Two v=spf1 records count as none.
    4. 
  
  4. Is mail signed (DKIM)? Your mail service gives you a DKIM record to add (a TXT or CNAME such as google._domainkey). Without it there is no signature to check.
    5. 
  
  5. Is there a DMARC record? A TXT record at _dmarc.yourdomain.com starting v=DMARC1. Gmail and Yahoo require one from anyone sending in volume, and its absence counts against you.
    6. 
  
  6. Does the From address match the site? A form that sends "From: the customer's own Gmail address" fails every check, because Gmail knows your server is not Gmail.
    7. 




Fix it, safest first


    
  
  1. Send through a real mail service. Install one SMTP plugin (WP Mail SMTP, FluentSMTP or Post SMTP) and connect it to the mailbox you already pay for (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Zoho) or a sending service (Brevo, Mailgun, SendGrid, Amazon SES). The plugin's test email should show "sent".
    2. 
  
  2. Fix SPF. Keep exactly one TXT record starting v=spf1, list each service with its own include: (for example include:_spf.google.com), and end it with ~all. Merge two records into one.
    3. 
  
  3. Turn on DKIM. Copy the DKIM record from the mail service into your DNS, then press "Verify" or "Start authentication" in the service's admin.
    4. 
  
  4. Add DMARC. Start gentle: a TXT record at _dmarc with v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@yourdomain.com. Move to p=quarantine once a couple of weeks of reports show your real mail passing.
    5. 
  
  5. Fix the form's From and Reply-To. From: an address on your own domain (for example website@yourdomain.com). Reply-To: the customer's address, so replying still reaches them.
    6. 
  
  6. Confirm. Send another test to Gmail and "Show original" again: SPF, DKIM and DMARC should all say PASS. DNS changes can take up to an hour to show.
    7.

What not to do


    
  
  • Do not add a second SPF record for the new service. Edit the one you have; two records break both.
    • 
  
  • Do not start DMARC at p=reject. One forgotten sender (an invoicing tool, a newsletter) would have its mail refused.
    • 
  
  • Do not ask customers to "check spam and mark as not spam". It helps that one inbox, not the next customer.
    • 
  
  • Do not send the form email "From" the visitor's address. That is exactly what spoofed mail looks like to Gmail.
    •
