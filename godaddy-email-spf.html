GoDaddy email with no SPF record: the 10-minute fix

In October 2026 I looked up the public email records of 19,113 small-business domains that receive mail. 1,017 of them use GoDaddy's own email (their mail servers are at secureserver.net). 83.2% of those publish no SPF record, the one line of DNS that tells Gmail and Outlook which servers may send email for the domain. Without it, anyone can send mail that claims to come from the business, and the business's own email is more likely to land in spam.

Microsoft 365 asks for the SPF record as a step of connecting a domain, which is likely why almost every Microsoft domain has one. The fix in GoDaddy DNS

This is for domains whose email is GoDaddy email (Workspace Email or the older cPanel mail). If you moved your email to Microsoft 365 or Google, use the check above instead; it gives the right value for your host.

  1. Sign in at godaddy.com, open My Products, and next to your domain choose DNS (or Manage DNS).
  2. Look through the TXT records for one whose value starts with v=spf1. If there is one, edit it rather than adding a second: a domain may have only one SPF record, and two count as a failure.
  3. Otherwise choose Add New Record and enter: 
    Type   TXT
Name   @
Value  v=spf1 include:secureserver.net ~all
TTL    1 Hour
  4. Add DMARC, which tells receivers what to do with mail that fails, and sends you a daily report of who is sending as you: 
    Type   TXT
Name   _dmarc
Value  v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:you@yourbusiness.com
TTL    1 Hour
    Put an address you actually read in place of you@yourbusiness.com.
  5. Wait an hour and run the check again. When the reports show every real sender passing (one to two weeks), change p=none to p=quarantine; that is the step that sends forged mail to spam.

Before you save, list everything that sends email as you. The website's contact form, an invoicing or booking system, a newsletter tool, a payment app. Each one needs its own include: in the same single SPF line, for example v=spf1 include:secureserver.net include:<the service's value> ~all. Leave one out and its genuine messages start failing once DMARC is tightened. The service's help pages name the include to use.

Why it matters now

  • Since February 2024 Gmail and Yahoo require SPF or DKIM on mail sent to their users. Without SPF, your own quotes and invoices are more likely to land in spam.
  • Invoice fraud starts with an email that looks like it came from you. With no SPF and no DMARC, nothing tells the receiver it did not.
  • It costs nothing: these are free DNS records at the domain company you already pay.
